**Likeliest cause of lingering ghosts: one-frame history. First test: finish the matched training extension already running.**

1. **Motion evidence.** B/C see one previous frame. A car’s last position alone cannot reveal its velocity, so copying it can reduce jitter while leaving a ghost after exit. GameNGen used 64 frames plus actions. Its largest context gain came from one to two frames (LPIPS 0.358 → 0.304). Our current cameras provide additional evidence, so the DOOM result supports a hypothesis about ghosting. [GameNGen, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14837v1#S5.T1)
2. **Training exposure and capacity.** We ran 4k updates × 16, with four history-free singles and six rows drawn from just 128 clips. E3 learns through rank-64 LoRA, camera adapter and history convolution with epsilon loss. GameNGen trained the full U-Net for 700k × 128 with velocity prediction. Growing history weights alone cannot establish undertraining. [GameNGen §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14837v1#S4.SS2)
3. **Error correction.** GameNGen teacher-forced noisy recorded frames with ten learned noise-level embeddings. B uses a continuous level plane. C adds six cached rollout predecessors per batch before dropout, refreshed at 1k/2.5k. Our rollout guides cameras at 2× with identical history in both passes and reuses drive noise. GameNGen guided history at 1.5×. Diffusion Forcing independently noises sequence tokens. [GameNGen §§3.2–3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14837v1#S3) · [Diffusion Forcing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01392)

**Decision.** Compare B against equal-step A at 6k/8k/12k on paired drives, locked s=0.3. Credit longer training only if ghosts, placement and late drift pass the signed gates alongside ID/W gains.
