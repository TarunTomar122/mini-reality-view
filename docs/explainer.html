Build the drone-view answer key

No real car has a drone filming it, so you make the answer images yourself. Give each lidar point the colour of the photo pixel it lands on, then look at the coloured points from a virtual camera 13 m behind and 8.5 m above the car.

3Data

Shrink everything into numbers

Models train much faster on compact numbers than on raw pixels. You convert everything once and save it to disk. Inputs and targets each get their own converter.

In · one camera photo (×6)
DINOv2-small frozen, you never train it
Out · a 32 × 18 grid of tokens Each square is one token: 384 numbers that describe that 14×14 patch (road, car edge, window…). Similar things get similar numbers, shown here as similar colours. The strip shows one token's 384 numbers.
In · one drone-view target
Stable Diffusion VAE encoder half
Out · a 32 × 18 × 4 "latent"
4 small maps of numbers instead of a full picture: 48× fewer numbers. The renderer learns to draw these. The VAE's decoder turns them back into a picture at the very end.
Photos
6 × 252×448×3 → 6 × 576 tokens × 384, saved as features/…/000123.pt
Target
144×256×3 (110,592 numbers) → 18×32×4 (2,304 numbers), saved as latents/…/000123.pt
Tesla
"Universal Vision Encoder" features, the same ones the driving model uses
4Training

Teach the renderer, one frame at a time

Take a real target, mix in a random amount of noise, and ask the model: given these camera tokens, which way leads back to the clean picture? After millions of examples, it can start from pure noise and walk step by step to a picture that matches the cameras. Drag the slider to see what the model is shown.

In · camera tokens + noisy target + noise level
t = 0.60
Renderer (DiT) ~30–130M parameters
Out · its guess at the clean frame The loss is how far the guess is from the truth. That error is what trains it.
noisy = (1 − t) · frame + t · noise ← "flow matching": the model learns the direction from noisy back to frame
Tesla
The same idea, a "mean-flow" model of about 1B parameters
5Training

Make it video

Same model with one extra input: the previous drone frame with a bit of noise on it. The noise stops it copying the old frame blindly, so small mistakes don't snowball. When it runs, each output becomes the next input. Press play.

6Data

Upgrade the answer key

Train on holey targets and the model learns to draw holes. So rebuild each 20-second scene as Gaussian splats (OmniRe, in the drivestudio repo): hundreds of thousands of coloured, fuzzy blobs fitted to match the photos. Render the splats from the drone camera, then redo stages 3–5 with these sharper targets.

In · the old lidar target The rebuild actually uses the photos, lidar and car positions of the whole scene.
OmniRe splat rebuild slow, but once per scene
Out · a splat scene + new targets
Out
One .ply splat file per scene, plus new 144×256×3 targets from any camera position you like
Tesla
Showed a fast "generative Gaussian splatting" rebuild in 2025
7Speed

Make it fast

Each denoising step is one full pass through the model, so fewer steps means more frames per second. Train the model to take bigger jumps (distillation, or a mean-flow model like Tesla's), make it smaller, and measure frames per second.

Goal
Faster than real time on one GPU at 144×256
Tesla
8 frames per run, played at 36 fps, on half of one AI accelerator chip in the car
8Testing

Test on streets it has never seen

Keep some scenes out of training. Run the model on them and compare against their real targets. The error map is bright wherever it's wrong. In this example it put one car in the wrong place and forgot another.

Scores
LPIPS: does each frame look different from the truth? FID: do frames look real overall? Then run it for 30 s and check whether it drifts.
