# Pivot conditioning r1: 6 cameras -> video model (Fable). Tags: [paper] = checked in a source today; [mem] = from memory, Neon please check; [est] = my estimate, unmeasured; [ours] = PROGRESS.md / repo.
Position change: in pivot-video-r1 I kept the CameraAdapter and made a warp the "second arm". Judged on merit, I now put the geometric guide first and the tokens second. Astra's answer not read (independent).
**1. Families and their best evidence.**
- **a) Geometric guide (unproject, render into the target camera, condition pixel-aligned).** GEN3C ([arXiv 2503.03751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03751), CVPR 2025): depth -> 3D cache -> renders along the target path as the condition; claims more precise camera control than pose-embedding methods and best sparse-view results, driving scenes included, with the argument "the model need not infer structure from the pose" [paper]. StreetCrafter ([2412.13188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13188)): coloured lidar renders as pixel-level conditions, holds up under lane changes on Waymo / PandaSet, but 0.2 fps [paper]. FreeVS ([2410.18079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18079), ICLR 2025): pseudo-image of projected coloured points -> real image, judged off-trajectory by 3D detectors [paper]. Uni3C ([2504.14899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14899)): point-cloud renders from monocular depth steer a frozen Wan 2.1 [paper]. ViewCrafter ([2409.02048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02048)), DriveDreamer4D ([2410.13571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13571)) [mem]. Caveat: the driving papers use LIDAR for the guide; camera-only depth is GEN3C / Uni3C / ViewCrafter.
- **b) Attention with camera embeddings.** CAT3D ([2405.10314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10314)) [mem]; Stable Virtual Camera ([2503.14489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14489)): Plücker-conditioned, large viewpoint changes, benchmark over 10 datasets incl. ViewCrafter as a baseline [paper]; ReCamMaster ([2503.11647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11647)): source video as in-context tokens beats reconstruct-then-fix, code not released [paper]; LVSM ([2410.17242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17242)): no 3D bias beats GS-LRM by 1.5-3.5 dB [paper]; MagicDrive-V2 ([2411.13807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13807)) [paper]. The catch: all of these learned the lifting from very large posed multi-view sets (LVSM: 64 A100 for 3-7 days [paper]; ReCamMaster: a purpose-built UE5 multi-camera set). We have about 35 minutes of unique video (21k ticks).
- **c) BEV / 3D lifting.** The most accurate published way to *locate* cars from these 6 cameras (BEVFormer, [2203.17270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17270)); [ours] camera-only BEVFormer boxes scored 0.417-0.437 hull F1 against 0.459 for ground-truth boxes (91-95%) while renderer E3 sat at 42% of its ceiling. But as an *interface to a generator* the driving papers do not hand over BEV tokens: they render the 3D state into the target view as pixel-aligned maps (SyntheOcc semantic multi-plane images [2410.00337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00337); Cosmos-Transfer control branches incl. lidar / HD-map videos [2503.14492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14492); Transfer2.5-2B auto / multiview: detector LET-AP 0.394 vs 0.243 for Transfer1-7B [project page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/cosmos-lab/cosmos-transfer2.5)) [paper]. So c) is a lifter that feeds a), not a rival input format. Costs: BEVFormer-base is far from 10 Hz on a 2080 Ti [est] and was trained on drives that overlap ours ([ours] leak flag: recall 0.848 train vs 0.724 unseen).
- **d) Hybrid.** TrajectoryCrafter ([2503.05638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05638), ICCV 2025): point renders (precise placement) + source video by attention (what the render misses); its ablation supports using both [paper]. This is the published answer to "warping leaves holes".
- **Reading.** Where data is scarce and placement is scored, pixel-aligned geometric guides win; attention-only wins when multi-view training data is effectively unlimited. Our data puts us firmly in the first case.
**2. Our geometry.**
- The scoring camera is 7.5 m behind, 2.4 m up, pitch -8 deg, 60 deg HFOV (mrv/carmap.py). The car cameras are about 1.5 m up [mem]. So the target is mostly the front three cameras pulled back about 9 m and lifted 0.9 m: it sees the same (rear) faces of cars, not a top-down view. This is a mild warp.
- A mild pull-back forgives depth error. For a point at front-camera depth d, a relative depth error e moves it in the chase view by f·x·e·d·b/(d+b)² with b = 9 m: at most a quarter of e. A 10% depth error is under ~8 px at the worst spot (d about 9 m, image edge) and 2-3 px for the road [my derivation, f = 443 px]. Cars beside us (side cameras) are worse, about 20 px for 10% at 4 m, on cars over 100 px wide. Depth flicker is damped by the same factor, so the guide is steady by construction.
- Attention has to learn that mapping from 21k ticks. [ours] it stalled: A@20k car F1 0.4456, no gain after ~20k steps. Partly self-inflicted: A sees 6 x 144 tokens of 28 x 28 px; a car at 40 m is about 15 px, half a token. A fair attention arm needs unpooled tokens (32 x 18 per view) and target-ray queries.
- [ours] The closed car-map branch (right map better on only 2/3 of keyframes at 1k updates) is not evidence against guides: it was bolted onto a model that already had camera tokens and stopped at 1k by rule. Lesson kept: the guide must be the main road from update 0.
- Depth at run time, camera-only: a small monocular net per view, scale fixed per camera per tick by the known camera height (road pixels must land on z = 0), then fine-tuned on train-drive lidar (lidar in training only). Candidates: Depth Anything V2-Small (24.8M, Apache-2.0; 60 ms / image on a V100 in its paper [paper]), Metric3D v2 ViT-S (11.6 fps on an A100 [paper]), UniDepthV2 ViT-S ([2502.20110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20110)), Depth Anything 3 Small / Base (takes known poses, Apache-2.0; Large / Giant are CC BY-NC [paper], [2511.10647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10647)). Too heavy per tick: Depth Pro, VGGT, MapAnything, feed-forward splats (STORM: 200 ms per reconstruction [paper], [2501.00602](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00602)). Cost [est]: 3 front views at 448x252 + 3 rear at half size, fp16, 30-50 ms / tick on a 2080 Ti. This is the family's main cost and must be timed first.
- Under the splat goes a flat-ground warp of the same views (one grid_sample, ~1 ms, hole-free, exact for the road). Past ticks fill road holes through the known ego pose, ground-height points only, so no car trails are painted in.
**3. How it enters, stays causal, and what it costs.**
- Guide per tick in the target view: RGB (splat over flat-ground warp) + valid mask + inverse depth + age. RGB goes through the backbone VAE; the rest is folded to the latent grid (4 ticks per latent, as Wan I2V does with its mask [mem]). All are concatenated to the noisy latent at the patch embedding with zero-initialised new input weights. No new tokens, so the DiT costs the same.
- Raw-view tokens: the depth net's own ViT features (one encoder pass serves both) + Plücker rays relative to the chase camera, projected into the slot where Wan reads text (umT5 dropped), zero-init gate. Cross-attention keys / values are computed once per tick, not per step. Kept only if they add car F1 in the ablation below.
- Training: new input weights + LoRA r64-128 on attention and MLPs; full fine-tune only as a control (overfit risk at 21k ticks). Guides are built with the run-time depth net, never lidar. Whole-guide dropout 20% + random hole masking (so tokens learn to fill), token dropout 10%, depth-scale and rig jitter (phone-photo goal).
- Causal: the guide for latent g uses only that group's 4 ticks and older, and rides in the input channels, so the KV cache holds past generated latents only and nothing is invalidated. Evidence that pixel-aligned control survives the streaming conversion: real-time VACE on autoregressive Wan 1.3B, depth / layout control at 17-22 FPS on a 5090 with 20-30% overhead for its side blocks ([2602.14381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14381)) [paper]; channel concat avoids that overhead.
- Backbone dependence. Wan 2.1 1.3B: as above; VACE-1.3B side blocks are a pretrained "control video -> video" entry worth a stage-0 look. Cosmos-Predict2 / 2.5 2B: same VAE; Transfer2.5 is this design already. LTX: its 32x VAE turns the guide into 16 x 8 cells and throws the precision away; one more reason against LTX.
- Live cost per tick, 2080 Ti [est]: depth 30-50 ms, splat + warp under 5 ms, guide VAE encode 10-20 ms (Wan encoder per 4-tick group, or a tiny encoder), tokens ~0 extra. About 45-75 ms against 13 ms for A's token path [ours]: 4-5x dearer, but small next to the DiT.
**4. Cheap test: E-cond, about 3.5 2080 Ti-hours, no video model.**
- Data: 48 train drives (~7k ticks) + the 6 dev drives. Build once: G = the guide above at 256 x 128 with estimated depth (timed on the 2080 Ti); G* = same with lidar depth (ceiling only); T = unpooled tokens of all 6 views + Plücker rays.
- Two readers of equal size (~10M) and budget (6k updates, seeds 0 / 1): reader-G a small conv U-Net on G; reader-T target-ray queries cross-attending T (the LVSM / CAT3D form). Both predict the key's car mask and road mask.
- Score on dev: car F1 (signed 0.3 matcher), road IoU, tick-to-tick mask jitter, near / far split at 30 m, paired drive bootstrap + LODO. Control: inputs from another drive must collapse both readers.
- Pre-registered reading: G - T car F1 >= +0.03 with CI excluding 0 and road IoU not lower -> guide first. T - G >= +0.03 -> attention first, no depth net at run time. Inside +-0.03 -> lower jitter wins; still tied -> tokens (cheaper). G* - G >= 0.05 -> depth is the limit: fine-tune it on lidar before the long run. Reader-T still improving at 6k -> rerun it to 12k before reading.
- Per the no-early-verdicts rule this only orders the arms. The stage-1 5k check confirms it on the real model: same checkpoint rendered guide-off and tokens-off (the dropouts make both valid).
**Risks.** The probe measures placement the input carries, not render quality. The keys are drawn from boxes and maps, so a photo-derived guide is a few px off the key. Depth cost may not fit 10 Hz on a 2080 Ti. Wrong rig calibration (phone photos) shifts the guide; Depth Anything 3 can estimate poses as a fallback [paper].

PROPOSAL: hybrid, geometry first — a camera-only chase-view guide (height-anchored monocular depth splat over a flat-ground warp, + mask / depth / age) channel-concatenated at the patch embedding, with the depth encoder's tokens + Plücker rays as a zero-init cross-attention residual in the text slot; LoRA + new inputs; E-cond decides the order before the long run.
